Selling a horse farm takes a different buyer.

A house buyer sees a big yard. A horse buyer walks straight to the barn, checks the arena footing, and asks how the water works in January. We market your farm to the people asking those questions.

  1. Walk the farm with us

    Barn, arena, fencing, pasture, hay storage, wells, and outbuildings. We note what horse buyers will ask about before they ask it.

  2. Price it against horse properties

    Ordinary home comps miss what a heated indoor arena or 20 fenced acres is worth. We price from equestrian sales.

  3. Shoot it in the right season

    Green pasture sells in June. A lit arena on a snowy evening sells in December. We plan photos and video for both.

  4. Put it in front of riders

    Boarding barns, trainers, breed and show communities, and our buyer email list, on top of the MLS.

  5. Close cleanly

    Well and septic inspections, surveys, animal-unit zoning, and moving the horses on closing day.

Built for a Minnesota winter

Buyers here ask about things a Florida listing never mentions. Every one of our listings answers them up front:

  • Heated or insulated barn
  • Heated waterers and frost-free hydrants
  • Indoor arena size, footing, and lighting
  • Plowed access to the barn and arena
  • Winter turnout and run-in sheds
